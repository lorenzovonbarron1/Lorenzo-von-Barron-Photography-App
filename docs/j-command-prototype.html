Optional experiment — not approved, not scheduled

Rail Zero

In v1 of this blueprint The Rail was the centerpiece and was described as improving table turnover. That was wrong: Handlebar J may not want faster turns, because people staying is the product. It is now one narrow experiment, and it has to earn its way in.

Status: awaiting management approval. Nothing on this screen runs until Ray, Joanne, a GM, or a management company approves it — and it runs on exactly one event when they do.
The one hypothesis it may test

That letting a guest request a timing window — Now · Next break · Second break · Last call — reduces the kitchen surge when the band stops, without harming the guest experience.

It may not claim faster service, better table turns, higher throughput, or an improved guest experience. Those are unproven or contrary to what the venue wants.

Boot Tag · cover-to-tab credit · song-triggered offers · The Round · zone routing · any Toast API work. These were v1's most interesting ideas. All of them are downstream of a question nobody has asked yet.

Success is not "guests used it." Success is that expo and the floor lead said the night was easier and no guest experience got worse. If guests love it and the kitchen hates it, the pilot failed — and the debrief form makes "this made the night harder" exactly as easy to record as "this helped."
